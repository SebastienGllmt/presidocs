Hello, world

This page runs on a shared blog engine, but its content lives in its own repository — separate from the engine and from every other blog built on it.

How it fits together

The engine is pulled in as a file: dependency. The narration player, the comment system, and the build pipeline all come from it; this repository contributes only posts, figures, and a small amount of configuration.

Everything around this text comes from the engine too: select a passage and a comment pill appears, the byline and footer render themselves, and if this post had generated audio, a narration player would sit in the corner reading it to you in the author's voice.

What you get without writing any of it

The comment system is the most visible piece. Select any passage in this article and a small pill offers to attach a comment to it; the card then anchors itself beside the exact text it discusses, tracking it as the page reflows. Readers sign in with an OAuth provider, and the author sees an unresolved-count badge plus per-thread resolution controls on top of the same surface.

Narration is the engine's signature feature. A post can carry a spoken script — the <script type="text/narration"> blocks in this file's head — and the generate step renders it to audio with a cloned voice, aligns every word, and emits a manifest the in-page player uses to highlight the sentence (and drive the figure) being spoken.

The same manifest feeds the subscription surfaces: an Atom feed for readers, a podcast RSS feed whose episodes are the narrated posts, and a per-post "copy as Markdown" twin so language models and note-taking tools get clean text instead of scraping the live DOM.

Figures like the pulsing dot above are content, not engine: they live in this repository's figures/ directory and register a journey the narrator and the offline video renderer can both drive. The engine gates them in CI for determinism, layout stability, and colour contrast, so an animated figure can't silently push the page around or fade its labels below legibility.

The build pipeline bundles each post into a self-contained page with content-hashed assets, generates social share cards in the blog's own typeface, injects structured data for crawlers, and emits a service worker that keeps previously-read posts available offline. All of it is static output a dumb edge can serve.

None of this requires configuration beyond the .env values the README walks through. The engine's design notes — why comments are a CRDT, why narration is a parallel narrative rather than a read-aloud, why the figures contract is forward-seek-only — live in its methodology.md, which doubles as the system's documentation.

Write content here; let the engine do the rest.
